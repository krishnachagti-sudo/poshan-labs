What is the waist-to-height ratio?

Your waist-to-height ratio is your waist measurement divided by your height, using the same unit for both. It tells you whether you're carrying too much fat around your tummy.

Written by Poshan Labs. Sources are named on the page. Last updated 26 October 2026.

Waist-to-height ratio: your waist measurement divided by your height, using the same unit for both.

What your waist-to-height ratio tells you

Carrying extra fat around your middle raises your risk of type 2 diabetes and heart disease.

Why it adds to BMI:

  • You can have a BMI in the healthy range and still carry extra fat at the waist, so this simple check can catch a risk that BMI misses.
  • The NHS uses it alongside BMI as a way of measuring health.

What moves your waist-to-height ratio

Belly fat responds to the same habits as overall fat loss:

  • fewer refined carbohydrates like maida, biscuits and mithai
  • more fibre from vegetables, whole grains and pulses
  • enough protein to keep you full
  • daily walking and some strength exercise
  • sleeping well and managing stress

Measure once a month at the same time of day, rather than every morning, so you see the real trend.

Is waist-to-height ratio on a report?

This isn't a blood test, though some check-up packages and dietitians record it. The NHS advises keeping your waist to less than half your height, which means a ratio under 0.5.

The NHS calculator isn't meant for:

  • pregnant women
  • people with an eating disorder
  • adults with a BMI over 35
