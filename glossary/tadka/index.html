What is tadka?

Tadka, also called chhaunk or tempering, is the step where whole spices like jeera, rai, hing, curry leaves or dried chillies are crackled in hot oil or ghee and poured over dal, sabzi or raita. It releases the flavour of the spices into the fat.

Where the flavour and the fat go in

Tadka is where much of the flavour of Indian food comes from, and it's also where much of the fat goes in.

The fats are not all the same:

  • Ghee and butter are high in saturated fat, which can raise LDL cholesterol.
  • Most vegetable oils, such as sunflower, are mainly unsaturated.

Every gram of fat carries about 9 kcal, more than twice as much as carbohydrate or protein, so a generous ladle of ghee in the tadka quietly adds a lot of calories.

A lighter tadka

Measure your oil with a spoon instead of pouring from the bottle. Let the spices do the work: a good tadka needs only a little fat to carry the flavour.

  • Rotate your cooking oils and keep ghee for small amounts.
  • Heat the oil just until the jeera sizzles, since smoking-hot oil burns the spices and makes them bitter.
  • One tadka over a big pot of dal goes further than extra fried toppings on each plate.
