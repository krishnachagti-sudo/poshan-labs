What is the waist-to-hip ratio?

Your waist-to-hip ratio is your waist measurement divided by your hip measurement. It shows whether your body stores more fat around the middle, an 'apple' shape, or around the hips and thighs, a 'pear' shape.

Written by Poshan Labs. Sources are named on the page. Last updated 16 October 2026.

Waist-to-hip ratio: your waist measurement divided by your hip measurement.

What your waist-to-hip ratio tells you

Fat around the waist is more closely linked with heart disease and other weight-related problems than fat on the hips.

Two women with the same weight can carry very different risks depending on where the fat sits.

What this ratio is:

  • It adds something your weight and BMI can't tell you.
  • It's a screening sign, not a diagnosis.

What moves your waist-to-hip ratio

You can't choose where you lose fat from, but overall fat loss usually trims the waist.

Habits that help:

  • Build meals around vegetables, dal, protein and whole grains.
  • Cut back on sugary drinks, mithai and fried namkeen.
  • Move every day.
  • Add strength exercise, which helps you keep muscle while you lose fat.

To measure:

  • Stand relaxed.
  • Measure your waist midway between the bottom of your ribs and the top of your hips (just above the belly button) after breathing out naturally.
  • Measure your hips at their widest point, in the same unit.
  • Divide the first by the second.

Is waist-to-hip ratio on a report?

It isn't a blood test, but some check-up summaries include it.

The World Health Organization (2008) treats these as a sign of fat stored around the middle:

  • 0.85 or more for women
  • 0.90 or more for men

These are the same cut-offs the waist-to-hip ratio calculator on this site uses. Other guidelines use slightly different numbers.

Checked against: WHO: Waist circumference and waist-hip ratio, report of an expert consultation (2008). General information, not medical advice.
