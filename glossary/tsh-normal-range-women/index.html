Glossary

TSH normal range for women, including pregnancy

A typical TSH range for adults starts at about 0.4 mIU/L, with an upper limit of about 4.0 to 4.5 mIU/L depending on the lab, and women are read against the same range when not pregnant. Pregnancy changes the limit, so your lab's pregnancy range, or the American Thyroid Association's fallback of about 4.0 mIU/L, is the one to use.

Written by Poshan Labs. Sources are named on the page. Last updated 16 October 2026.

What your number means

These are for adults who are not pregnant, against a usual range from about 0.4 mIU/L to an upper limit of 4.0 to 4.5.

They help you read a report, and they do not replace your doctor.

The ranges

Labs print slightly different limits, and the range on your own report is the one your doctor uses.

The American Thyroid Association does not give one fixed number per trimester in 2017. It asks for a range made for pregnant women, and gives fallbacks when there is none.

Adults, not pregnantFrom about 0.4, upper limit 4.0 to 4.5 mIU/LA typical laboratory reference interval. The upper limit is about 4.0 to 4.5 depending on the lab, and the range on your report is the one that applies to you.ATA/AACE 2012 guideline, Korevaar, Thyroid Research 2018

Things to keep in mind

  • TSH is the hormone your brain makes to tell your thyroid how hard to work, so a high TSH usually means the thyroid is underactive and a low one usually means it is overactive.
  • If your thyroid is underactive, the tablet your doctor prescribes is what corrects TSH, and food cannot replace it. Take it exactly as advised, and ask your doctor how far apart to keep it from meals, chai and other medicines.
  • Reference ranges vary somewhat by laboratory, age and pregnancy status, and illness and some medicines can also move TSH. That is one more reason to read it with your doctor.
