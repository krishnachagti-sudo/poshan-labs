Glossary

What is sattu?

The Indian food tables (IFCT 2017) have no entry for sattu, and roasting changes the numbers of the chana it is made from, so this page doesn't give a calorie or protein number for it. Sattu is a flour made by dry-roasting and grinding Bengal gram (chana), sometimes mixed with roasted barley or other grains.

Sattu: a flour made by dry-roasting and grinding Bengal gram (chana), sometimes mixed with roasted barley or other grains.

It's a traditional food in Bihar, Jharkhand, eastern Uttar Pradesh and Punjab, taken as a drink, used as a stuffing for litti and parathas, or eaten as a quick meal.

Sattu and your health

Because it comes from a pulse, sattu gives you protein and fibre, along with iron and folate, in a form that needs almost no cooking.

Sattu is a useful swap when you'd otherwise reach for biscuits or a refined snack, because:

  • pulses digest slowly
  • their protein and fibre help you feel full

Research also links eating pulses regularly with lower LDL cholesterol.

How to have sattu

A glass of sattu stirred into water with lemon, roasted jeera and a pinch of salt makes a filling summer drink, and it doesn't need sugar.

You can also:

  • mix some into your roti atta for extra protein
  • use it as a paratha stuffing with onion, green chilli and coriander

Keep the portion about the same as a katori of dal, and pair it with vegetables. If pulses give you wind, start with small amounts and build up slowly.
