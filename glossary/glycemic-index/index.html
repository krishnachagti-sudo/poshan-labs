What is the glycemic index?

The glycemic index (GI) is a score from 0 to 100 that shows how quickly a food containing carbohydrate raises your blood sugar. Pure glucose scores 100.

Written by Poshan Labs. Sources are named on the page. Last updated 17 October 2026.

Glycemic index: a score from 0 to 100 that shows how quickly a food containing carbohydrate raises your blood sugar.

Foods with little or no carbohydrate, like oil, ghee, meat or eggs, don't have a GI.

Low, medium and high GI

If you have diabetes, prediabetes or PCOS, choosing more low and medium GI foods can help keep your sugar steadier.

GI bands:

  • Low-GI foods (55 or below) raise blood sugar slowly.
  • Medium-GI foods (56 to 69) raise it moderately.
  • High-GI foods (70 and above) raise it quickly.

But GI isn't everything: portion size and total carbohydrate still matter, and some high-GI foods are nutritious.

What changes a food's GI

Processing, cooking and ripeness all change a food's GI, and foods higher in fat and fibre tend to score lower. Juice scores higher than whole fruit, and very soft-cooked grains higher than firmer ones.

Tend to be low:

  • barley and whole oats
  • dals and beans
  • most fruit
  • milk and dahi

Tend to be high:

  • white rice and potatoes
  • white bread and instant cereals
  • sugar

You can balance a higher-GI food by pairing it with lower-GI ones, like rice with plenty of dal and vegetables. Talk to your doctor or dietitian before relying on GI to manage diabetes.
