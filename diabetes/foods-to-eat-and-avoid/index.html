Foods to eat and avoid in diabetes, for sugar patients

If you or someone at home is a sugar patient, the pattern is simple: build your meals on dals, millets, curd and vegetables, and keep sugar, sugary drinks, maida, namkeen and fried snacks small.

Numbers worked out by Poshan Labs from IFCT 2017 (ICMR-NIN) and USDA FoodData Central, using the rules on How the ratings work. Last updated 16 October 2026.

Why build on these

Each of these keeps the rise in sugar slow: its fibre is high for its carbohydrate, or a serving has very little carbohydrate at all. One serving of each:

Why keep these small

  • Added sugar, in chai and coffee too Jaggery and honey count as added sugar as well. It brings calories and nothing else, and the guidelines call more than 25 g a day high. (Guideline 15, p. 94)
  • Mithai and other sweets Sugar and fat together, often fried or made with ghee. The guidelines cover them as sugary and fatty foods. (Guideline 15, p. 99)
  • Sugary drinks, sharbat and packaged fruit juice Sugar in a glass, without the fibre of the whole fruit. The guidelines prefer whole fruit even to fresh juice. (Guideline 14, pp. 90-92; Guideline 15, p. 99)
  • Biscuits, cake, rusk and other bakery Usually maida with added sugar and fat, all three at once. (Guideline 15, p. 99)
  • White bread, pav, naan and anything else made with maida The bran is milled away: per 100 g, maida has 2.8 g of fibre and atta 11.4 g. The guidelines list it as a refined flour. (Guideline 15, pp. 97-98)
  • Namkeen, bhujia and chips Fried and salty, and easy to eat by the handful without noticing. The guidelines name namkeen and chips among foods high in salt. (Guideline 15, p. 94)
  • Fried snacks: samosa, pakora, kachori, bhature Deep-fried, so a single piece carries a lot of oil. The guidelines count all deep-fried foods, samosa and kachori among them, as high in fat. (Guideline 15, p. 93)
  • White rice About 31.3 g of carbohydrate in a 40 g serving, with very little fibre to slow it. Compare: Rice vs roti, Brown rice vs white rice, Dalia vs rice.
  • Poha (rice flakes) About 30.7 g of carbohydrate in a 40 g serving, with very little fibre to slow it.
  • Dates (khajoor) 16 g of sugar in a 24 g serving.
  • Oils and ghee One teaspoon (5 g) is about 44 kcal, so measure it.

The ICMR-NIN Dietary Guidelines for Indians (2024) ask everyone to keep foods high in fat, sugar and salt, and ultra-processed foods, to a minimum, and to eat whole fruit rather than juice (Guideline 14, pp. 90-92, and Guideline 15, pp. 93-99). Food numbers from the Indian Food Composition Tables 2017, National Institute of Nutrition (ICMR), except the 2 marked USDA, which come from USDA FoodData Central because the Indian tables don't list them.

Questions people ask

Can a diabetic eat banana?

Yes, in moderation. About 23.6 g of carbohydrate in a 100 g serving, 13.7 g of it sugar, so keep to this portion. All about Banana (Kela).

Can a diabetic eat apple?

Yes, in moderation. About 19.7 g of carbohydrate in a 150 g serving, 14.3 g of it sugar, so keep to this portion. All about Apple (Sev).

Can a diabetic eat sweet corn?

Yes, in moderation. The local bhutta (22.7 g of carbohydrate in one medium cob, about 100 g of kernels) reads “Eat in moderation”. For 100 g of sweet corn (the tables list it as Maize, tender, sweet), it reads “Good choice”: 3.3 g of fibre for 16.4 g of carbohydrate, which slows the rise in sugar. All about Tender maize (bhutta).

Can a diabetic eat dates?

Keep it small. 16 g of sugar in a 24 g serving. All about Dates (khajoor).

Can a diabetic eat papaya?

Yes, it is a good choice. Only 4.6 g of carbohydrate in a 100 g serving. All about Papaya (Papita).

Can a diabetic eat pineapple?

Yes, in moderation. About 9.4 g of carbohydrate in a 100 g serving, 8.6 g of it sugar, so keep to this portion. All about Pineapple (Ananas).

Can a diabetic eat poha?

Keep it small. About 30.7 g of carbohydrate in a 40 g serving, with very little fibre to slow it. All about Poha (rice flakes).

Can a diabetic eat pomegranate?

Yes, in moderation. About 11.6 g of carbohydrate in a 100 g serving, 10.9 g of it sugar, so keep to this portion. All about Pomegranate (Anar).

Can a diabetic eat sweet potato?

Yes, in moderation. About 24.2 g of carbohydrate in a 100 g serving, so watch the portion. All about Sweet potato (Shakarkand).

Can a diabetic eat rice?

Keep it small. About 31.3 g of carbohydrate in a 40 g serving, with very little fibre to slow it. All about White rice.
