High protein vegetarian foods

If you do not eat meat, fish or eggs, these are the foods that carry the most protein, ranked from the food tables and worked out for a real serving.

Numbers worked out by Poshan Labs from IFCT 2017 (ICMR-NIN) and USDA FoodData Central. Last updated 16 October 2026.

Ranked by what one normal serving gives, because a serving is what you eat, not 100 g.

The top five:

  1. soya chunks (15.4 g in 30 g dry, about one katori once soaked)
  2. soya bean (10.7 g in one katori cooked)
  3. paneer (9.4 g in a 50 g piece)
  4. green tuvar (8.1 g in one katori)
  5. buffalo milk (7.4 g in one glass)

Things to know before you use this list

  • Spoon foods: This list leaves out foods you eat by the pinch or the spoon, such as fenugreek seeds, halim seeds, poppy seeds and mustard seeds, because this list compares foods you eat by the katori or the piece, and per 100 g the spoon foods would top it. Fenugreek seeds have 25.4 g of protein per 100 g, and a pinch to half a teaspoon (2 g) gives 0.5 g. See its page.
  • Raw or dry: these are the values for the food as the tables measured it, raw or dry. Cooking changes them: dals and grains soak up water, and boiling vegetables moves some minerals into the water you throw away.
  • Vegetarian here means no meat, fish or egg. Milk, curd and paneer are included. There is no vitamin B12 list, because the IFCT data the site uses has no B12 values, so there is nothing honest to rank. What B12 does and where it comes from is in the B12 note.
