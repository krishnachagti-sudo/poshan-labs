Moong dal vs masoor dal: which is better?

Moong dal and masoor dal side by side, goal by goal, with the numbers behind each rating.

Numbers worked out by Poshan Labs from IFCT 2017 (ICMR-NIN), using the rules on How the ratings work. Last updated 17 October 2026.

No winner on the ratings: moong dal and masoor dal come out level for all four goals.

Where they differ, per 100 g, dry and before cooking:

  • Moong dal: 326 kcal, 23.9 g of protein and 9.4 g of fibre
  • Masoor dal: 322 kcal, 24.4 g of protein and 10.4 g of fibre

Which is better, goal by goal

Weight loss They rate the same for weight loss.

They rate the same for weight loss. Per 100 g, dry and before cooking, moong dal has 326 kcal and 9.4 g of fibre; masoor dal has 322 kcal and 10.4 g of fibre. Masoor dal has more fibre. The rating goes by a normal serving, and at a serving the difference is not enough to put either ahead.

  • Moong dal, one katori cooked (30 g dry): good choice. 2.9 g of fibre per 100 kcal keeps you full for longer.
  • Masoor dal, one katori cooked (30 g dry): good choice. 3.2 g of fibre per 100 kcal keeps you full for longer.
PCOS and diabetes They rate the same for PCOS and diabetes.

They rate the same for PCOS and diabetes. Per 100 g, dry and before cooking, moong dal has 52.6 g of carbohydrate and 9.4 g of fibre; masoor dal has 52.5 g of carbohydrate and 10.4 g of fibre. Masoor dal has more fibre. The rating goes by a normal serving, and at a serving the difference is not enough to put either ahead.

  • Moong dal, one katori cooked (30 g dry): good choice. 2.8 g of fibre for 15.8 g of carbohydrate, which slows the rise in sugar.
  • Masoor dal, one katori cooked (30 g dry): good choice. 3.1 g of fibre for 15.8 g of carbohydrate, which slows the rise in sugar.
Menopause They rate the same for menopause.

They rate the same for menopause. Per 100 g, dry and before cooking, moong dal has 43.1 mg of calcium and 23.9 g of protein; masoor dal has 44.3 mg of calcium and 24.4 g of protein. These numbers are very close.

  • Moong dal, one katori cooked (30 g dry): good choice. 7.2 g of protein in a 30 g serving helps you hold on to muscle.
  • Masoor dal, one katori cooked (30 g dry): good choice. 7.3 g of protein in a 30 g serving helps you hold on to muscle.

What is in each

What is in each, so you can compare the ingredients as well as the totals:

  • Moong dal, per katori: moong dal 30 g, onion 15 g, tomato 20 g, groundnut oil 3 g, turmeric powder 1 g and cumin seeds 1 g.
  • Masoor dal, per katori: masoor dal 30 g, onion 15 g, tomato 20 g, groundnut oil 3 g and turmeric powder 1 g.

The weights are raw, as the tables measure them, and the oil is the oil in the recipe. Your cooking will differ a little, so treat these as a starting point.

How this was decided

  • Same rule: each goal uses the same rule as every food page, so this page cannot disagree with them. How the ratings work shows the thresholds.
  • Serving: each food is rated at its own normal serving, as on its own page: the same serving for both, one katori cooked (30 g dry).
  • On its own: a rating is about the food on its own; the oil it is cooked in, what else is on the plate and your own reports change the real answer.
  • Too close: where two ratings differ only because the numbers that decide them sit within 10% of each other, either side of a threshold, the page calls it too close rather than name a winner.

Read each food on its own page: Moong dal and Masoor dal. Ask Puja or your doctor before you swap one for the other if you take medicine for diabetes or thyroid.
