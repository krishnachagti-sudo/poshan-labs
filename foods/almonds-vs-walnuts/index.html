Which is better, goal by goal

Weight loss and PCOS They rate the same for weight loss and PCOS.

They rate the same for weight loss and PCOS. Per 100 g, almonds have 609 kcal, 13.1 g of fibre and 3 g of carbohydrate; walnuts have 671 kcal, 5.4 g of fibre and 10.1 g of carbohydrate. Almonds have more fibre; walnuts have more calories and carbohydrate. The rating goes by a normal serving, and at a serving the difference is not enough to put either ahead.

  • Almonds, a small handful (15 g): eat in moderation. A small handful (15 g) is about 91 kcal, so keep it to that.
  • Walnuts, a small handful (15 g): eat in moderation. A small handful (15 g) is about 101 kcal, so keep it to that.
Diabetes They rate the same for diabetes.

They rate the same for diabetes. Per 100 g, almonds have 3 g of carbohydrate and 13.1 g of fibre; walnuts have 10.1 g of carbohydrate and 5.4 g of fibre. Almonds have more fibre; walnuts have more carbohydrate. The rating goes by a normal serving, and at a serving the difference is not enough to put either ahead.

  • Almonds, a small handful (15 g): good choice. Only 0.5 g of carbohydrate in a 15 g serving.
  • Walnuts, a small handful (15 g): good choice. Only 1.5 g of carbohydrate in a 15 g serving.
Menopause They rate the same for menopause.

They rate the same for menopause. Per 100 g, almonds have 228 mg of calcium and 18.4 g of protein; walnuts have 105 mg of calcium and 14.9 g of protein. Almonds have more calcium and protein. The rating goes by a normal serving, and at a serving the difference is not enough to put either ahead.

  • Almonds, a small handful (15 g): fine as part of a meal. 34 mg of calcium and 2.8 g of protein in a 15 g serving, not enough to call it a good source, but fine as part of a meal.
  • Walnuts, a small handful (15 g): fine as part of a meal. 2.2 g of protein in a 15 g serving, not enough to call it a good source, but fine as part of a meal.

How this was decided

  • Same rule: each goal uses the same rule as every food page, so this page cannot disagree with them. How the ratings work shows the thresholds.
  • Serving: each food is rated at its own normal serving, as on its own page: the same serving for both, a small handful (15 g).
  • On its own: a rating is about the food on its own; the oil it is cooked in, what else is on the plate and your own reports change the real answer.
  • Too close: where two ratings differ only because the numbers that decide them sit within 10% of each other, either side of a threshold, the page calls it too close rather than name a winner.
