How the ratings work

The rules behind every rating on the food pages, with the exact numbers.

Every food page rates the food for four goals, worked out for a normal serving from the food's own numbers.

  • Where the numbers come from: the Indian Food Composition Tables 2017, National Institute of Nutrition (ICMR), or USDA FoodData Central for the few foods the Indian tables lack.
  • Who picks the rating: no person does. The rules pick every rating, so a rating can't know your reports. That part happens on your call.

Puja is reviewing the thresholds in the table below. Until she has read a kind of page herself, those pages say the numbers were worked out by Poshan Labs, not that she wrote them.

What decides the rating

PCOS and PCODBoth the weight loss and the diabetes rules rate it good, or the diabetes rule rates it good and a serving has at least 8 g of proteinEither of those rules says limit
MenopauseAt least 100 mg of calcium or 6 g of protein per serving, with 10 g of sugar or lessMore than 15 g of sugar per serving
Oils and gheeNeverAlways, with the calories in one teaspoon
Sugar and jaggeryNeverAlways
Maida (refined flour)NeverFor weight loss, PCOS and diabetes, because the bran is milled away
  • In between: anything in between is Eat in moderation.
  • Menopause: a food that is fine but not a standout for calcium or protein reads Fine as part of a meal; one held back by its sugar still reads Eat in moderation.
  • Missing numbers: a number that was never measured is left out, never counted as zero.

Exceptions

  • Nuts and oil seeds: always Eat in moderation for weight loss: good food, but a serving is a lot of calories.
  • Dense sweet foods: foods over 150 kcal per 100 g that get 40% or more of their calories from sugar, like dates and raisins, are Eat in moderation for weight loss too, even with their fibre.
  • PCOS: also uses the weight-loss rule, so nuts and seeds read Eat in moderation there too, and the sweet ones Eat in moderation or Limit. For diabetes and menopause they are rated from their numbers.
  • Spices: Fine in small amounts, because a pinch never changes your day.
  • By the spoon: mustard and garden cress (halim) seeds, used a teaspoon at a time, and tamarind and lemon juice, used by the spoon for sourness, are rated like spices too.
  • Onion: the one item from the spice shelf rated as a vegetable, because you eat it by the onion, not the pinch.
  • Khoa: held at Eat in moderation for diabetes because 59% of its calories come from fat, even though a 20 g piece has only 3.3 g of carbohydrate.
  • Iron: not part of the menopause rule. The NIH Office of Dietary Supplements lists 18 mg of iron a day for women aged 19 to 50 and 8 mg for adults 51 and older. If you still have periods, iron still matters.

Servings

Servings are household amounts, set food by food. Each food page names its own. The usual ones:

  • Cooked rice: one katori (40 g raw)
  • Atta: one roti's worth (30 g)
  • Cooked dal: a katori (30 g dry)
  • Most vegetables and fruit: 100 g
  • Nuts: a small handful (15 g)
  • Oils and sugar: one teaspoon (5 g)
  • Milk: one glass (200 g)
