Brown rice vs white rice: which is better?

Brown rice and white rice side by side, goal by goal, with the numbers behind each rating.

Numbers worked out by Poshan Labs from IFCT 2017 (ICMR-NIN), using the rules on How the ratings work. Last updated 16 October 2026.

Brown rice is the better pick for PCOS and diabetes. For weight loss and menopause they rate the same.

Per 100 g, dry and before cooking:

  • Brown rice: 354 kcal, 9.2 g of protein and 4.4 g of fibre
  • White rice: 356 kcal, 7.9 g of protein and 2.8 g of fibre

Which is better, goal by goal

Weight loss They rate the same for weight loss.

They rate the same for weight loss. Per 100 g, dry and before cooking, brown rice has 354 kcal and 4.4 g of fibre; white rice has 356 kcal and 2.8 g of fibre. Brown rice has more fibre. The rating goes by a normal serving, and at a serving the difference is not enough to put either ahead.

  • Brown rice, one katori cooked (40 g dry): eat in moderation. 141 kcal in a 40 g serving, without much fibre or protein to fill you up.
  • White rice, one katori cooked (40 g dry): eat in moderation. 143 kcal in a 40 g serving, without much fibre or protein to fill you up.
PCOS and diabetes Brown rice is the better pick for PCOS and diabetes.

Brown rice is the better pick for PCOS and diabetes.

  • Brown rice, one katori cooked (40 g dry): eat in moderation. About 29.9 g of carbohydrate in a 40 g serving, so watch the portion.
  • White rice, one katori cooked (40 g dry): limit. About 31.3 g of carbohydrate in a 40 g serving, with very little fibre to slow it.
Menopause They rate the same for menopause.

They rate the same for menopause. Per 100 g, dry and before cooking, brown rice has 10.9 mg of calcium and 9.2 g of protein; white rice has 7.5 mg of calcium and 7.9 g of protein. Brown rice has more calcium and protein. The rating goes by a normal serving, and at a serving the difference is not enough to put either ahead.

  • Brown rice, one katori cooked (40 g dry): fine as part of a meal. 3.7 g of protein in a 40 g serving, not enough to call it a good source, but fine as part of a meal.
  • White rice, one katori cooked (40 g dry): fine as part of a meal. 3.2 g of protein in a 40 g serving, not enough to call it a good source, but fine as part of a meal.

How this was decided

  • Same rule: each goal uses the same rule as every food page, so this page cannot disagree with them. How the ratings work shows the thresholds.
  • Serving: each food is rated at its own normal serving, as on its own page: the same serving for both, one katori cooked (40 g dry).
  • On its own: a rating is about the food on its own; the oil it is cooked in, what else is on the plate and your own reports change the real answer.
  • Too close: where two ratings differ only because the numbers that decide them sit within 10% of each other, either side of a threshold, the page calls it too close rather than name a winner.

Read each food on its own page: Brown rice and White rice. Ask Puja or your doctor before you swap one for the other if you take medicine for diabetes or thyroid.
