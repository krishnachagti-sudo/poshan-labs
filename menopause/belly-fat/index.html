Menopause

Menopause belly fat: what actually helps

Belly fat in menopause is real. As oestrogen falls, the body stores more fat around the middle, and the muscle you have been losing since your thirties burns fewer calories. Food helps most when it keeps protein up and portions honest. Add strength training twice a week to your walking, sleep well, and measure your waist, not just your weight.

Why the fat moves to your middle

The Menopause Society says ageing is the main reason weight creeps up in midlife, but menopause changes where it goes.

  • As oestrogen drops, the body stores more fat in the abdomen (The Menopause Society).
  • Women also lose muscle at 3 to 8% a decade after the age of 30 (The Menopause Society).
  • Less muscle means fewer calories burned at rest, even if you eat exactly what you always did (The Menopause Society).

Moving less, stress, poor sleep and hot flushes all add to it (The Menopause Society). So this is not you being lazy. Your body has changed, and your food needs to change with it.

What it means for health

This is about health, not only looks. Extra weight around the middle raises the risk of:

  • high blood pressure
  • diabetes
  • abnormal cholesterol
  • fat in the liver (The Menopause Society).

The ICMR-NIN guidelines make the same point: fat in and around the organs is linked with lifestyle diseases (ICMR-NIN guidelines). The glossary has more on visceral fat.

Measure your waist, not just your weight

NICE, the UK's health guidance body, says the aim is to keep your waist to less than half your height (NICE).

It says people of South Asian background are prone to fat around the middle (NICE).

How to measure

Measure your waist midway between the bottom of your ribs and the top of your hips, just above the belly button, after breathing out naturally (NICE).

The waist-to-height ratio tool does the sum for you.

How to build the plate in menopause

Protein comes first, because it helps you hold on to muscle (The Menopause Society), so some goes into every meal.

The Menopause Society recommends a diet rich in:

  • fruits
  • vegetables
  • lean proteins
  • whole grains (The Menopause Society).

In an Indian kitchen that means a katori of dal, chana or rajma, or paneer, egg, chicken or fish, with every main meal.

The protein calculator gives you your own daily number.

Then fibre and volume. The ICMR-NIN guidelines ask for:

  • at least 400 g of vegetables a day (ICMR-NIN guidelines)
  • whole grains and millets over refined ones (ICMR-NIN guidelines).

Half the thali goes to sabzi. The roti stays, measured.

Calcium and bones

Your bones need attention now too. Ragi, paneer and til are all rated a good choice for menopause on their food pages for the calcium in a normal serving.

The calcium-rich foods without milk list is there if you don't take milk.

Sugar, oil and fried food

What to cut back first is what the guidelines name:

  • sugar
  • extra oil
  • deep-fried snacks
  • refined foods (ICMR-NIN guidelines).

In practice that is the extra spoon of ghee, the sweet chai and the evening namkeen.

Move, and lift something

The Menopause Society suggests:

  • at least 150 minutes of moderate aerobic activity a week (The Menopause Society)
  • strength training twice a week (The Menopause Society).

Brisk walking counts for the first part. For the second, these are all ways to work your muscles:

  • squats against a wall
  • a resistance band
  • weights at the gym.

The ICMR-NIN guidelines also tie resistance exercise to keeping muscle (ICMR-NIN guidelines). If you have joint or heart trouble, ask your doctor what is safe before you start.

Sleep and stress

Sleep and stress count as well. The Menopause Society links poor sleep and high stress to weight gain (The Menopause Society).

The ICMR-NIN guidelines suggest 6 to 8 hours of sleep a night to avoid unhealthy eating and weight gain (ICMR-NIN guidelines).

Hormone therapy and medicines

Hormone therapy is not a weight-loss treatment. The Menopause Society says it eases hot flushes, poor sleep and mood swings, which makes the rest easier (The Menopause Society).

It also says some studies suggest a modest effect on belly fat (The Menopause Society).

With your doctor

Whether it suits you is between you and your doctor. A food plan works alongside whatever they prescribe.

What to keep small

Nothing here is banned. They are where the calories hide. The reason after each food on this site is its own rating for weight loss.

  • Mustard oil, or any cooking oilLimitOne teaspoon (5 g) is about 44 kcal, so measure it.
  • Gud (jaggery)LimitIt is still sugar.4.2 g of sugar in one teaspoon (5 g).

Questions people ask

Why am I gaining belly fat in menopause?

Falling oestrogen moves fat to the abdomen, and the muscle you lose with age burns fewer calories at rest (The Menopause Society).

Can you lose belly fat after menopause?

It can be managed. The Menopause Society says that if weight is already a problem, losing even 5 to 10% of your body weight can:

  • improve your health
  • lower the risk of chronic disease (The Menopause Society).

What exercise helps menopause belly fat?

The Menopause Society suggests at least 150 minutes a week of moderate activity such as brisk walking, plus strength training twice a week (The Menopause Society).

Should I stop eating roti in menopause?

No. The Menopause Society counts whole grains as part of a balanced diet (The Menopause Society). Keep the roti, measure it, and put more sabzi and dal next to it.

How much protein do I need in menopause?

Enough to help you hold on to muscle. The Menopause Society says protein helps preserve muscle mass (The Menopause Society).

Spread it across every meal. The protein calculator works out your own daily number from your weight.
