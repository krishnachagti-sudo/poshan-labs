Get this chart

Every sample day has at least 55 g of protein.

ICMR-NIN's 2020 allowance is 0.83 g per kg a day, about 54 g for its 65 kg reference man and 46 g for its 55 kg reference woman. The protein calculator gives your own number.

How the numbers were worked out

Calories and nutrients are calculated from the Indian Food Composition Tables 2017, National Institute of Nutrition (ICMR), except curd, which uses USDA FoodData Central SR Legacy 171284 (plain whole-milk yogurt) because the Indian tables don't list it. Fibre is IFCT's total dietary fibre, which counts more of a food's fibre than the crude fibre in older Indian tables, so a day reads higher than you may expect. Cooked portions assume home cooking with the oil listed in each dish. Katori means a standard 150 ml bowl.

Why the day looks like this

This day is built around smaller, evenly spaced portions of carbohydrate, with protein and fibre at every main meal.

It keeps everyday Indian food at the centre, so there's nothing on it you'd struggle to cook or find.

This is a sample, not your plan

A sample day shows what eating for diabetes can look like. It can't know your reports, your medicines, your routine or what you can't stand eating.

Your own plan starts from all of those, and it changes every week as Puja sees how your body responds.

Medicines, pregnancy and breastfeeding

If you take medicine for diabetes or thyroid, or you're pregnant or breastfeeding, don't switch to a sample plan on your own. Talk to your doctor, or to Puja on a free call.

On insulin or sugar tablets

If you take insulin or tablets that lower sugar, eating less or changing meal times can bring on low sugar: shakiness, sweating, confusion. Don't change calories or skip meals without your doctor.
